AREDS2: The Only Eye Formula Backed by an NIH Clinical Trial

Most eye health supplements make vague claims. AREDS2 is different — it's the outcome of a large, government-funded clinical trial with a specific, tested nutrient combination.

📋 Health Reviews Labs Editorial Team · Updated July 2026

Age-related Macular Degeneration (AMD) is the leading cause of vision loss in adults over 60 in developed countries. The AREDS2 trial, funded by the U.S. National Eye Institute, remains the most rigorously tested nutritional protocol for slowing its progression — and it's worth understanding exactly what it found, because most supplements marketed for eye health don't actually follow it.

Quick Answer

  • AREDS2 (Age-Related Eye Disease Study 2) was a large NIH-funded clinical trial testing a specific nutrient combination's effect on AMD progression.
  • The tested formula replaced beta-carotene (linked to increased lung cancer risk in smokers) with lutein and zeaxanthin, two carotenoids naturally concentrated in the retina.
  • The trial found the updated formula reduced progression to advanced AMD by about 25% over five years in people with intermediate AMD — it does not prevent AMD or restore vision already lost.

Why AREDS2 Replaced the Original AREDS Formula

The original 2001 AREDS trial included beta-carotene, which showed benefit for AMD progression but was later linked to a significantly increased risk of lung cancer in smokers and former smokers in separate large trials. The follow-up AREDS2 trial (results published in 2013) tested replacing beta-carotene with lutein and zeaxanthin — carotenoids that occur naturally in the macula and don't carry the same cancer-risk signal.

The trial found that the lutein/zeaxanthin substitution performed at least as well as beta-carotene for AMD progression, without the associated risk — which is why any credible eye health formula referencing AREDS should be using the AREDS2 nutrient profile, not the original 2001 version.

What the Trial Actually Found (and Didn't)

The AREDS2 formula was tested specifically in people who already had intermediate AMD or advanced AMD in one eye — it is not a prevention protocol for people with healthy eyes, and it does not reverse existing vision loss. What it demonstrated was a meaningful reduction, roughly 25%, in the rate of progression to advanced AMD (the stage associated with significant vision loss) over five years of follow-up.

This distinction matters because it separates AREDS2 from most eye supplement marketing, which often implies general vision improvement or prevention. The actual clinical evidence supports a much more specific claim: slowing progression in people who already have measurable AMD.

Who Should Actually Consider an AREDS2-Formula Supplement

Anyone diagnosed with intermediate or advanced (in one eye) AMD by an eye care professional is the population the research actually supports. For people without a diagnosis, or with only early AMD, the evidence for benefit is considerably weaker — an eye exam with a professional is the only reliable way to know which category applies, since AMD in its early stages is often symptomless.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the AREDS2 formula exactly?
A specific nutrient combination — Vitamin C, Vitamin E, Zinc, Copper, Lutein, and Zeaxanthin — tested in a large NIH-funded clinical trial for its effect on macular degeneration progression.
Does AREDS2 prevent macular degeneration?
No. The trial studied people who already had intermediate or advanced (one eye) AMD, and found it slowed progression by about 25% over five years. It's not a prevention formula for healthy eyes.
Why was beta-carotene removed from the original formula?
Beta-carotene was linked to significantly increased lung cancer risk in smokers and former smokers in separate large trials, so AREDS2 tested replacing it with lutein and zeaxanthin, which performed comparably without that risk.
Who should consider an AREDS2-based supplement?
Primarily people diagnosed with intermediate or advanced (one eye) AMD by an eye care professional — the clinical evidence is specific to this population, not the general public.
